# Day 9 – Negatives: *Tidak* vs *Bukan* 🚫

Today, you’ll learn how to say **“no” or “not”** in Indonesian using just two simple words:

- **tidak** → for **verbs** and **adjectives**
- **bukan** → for **nouns** or when **correcting**

This is important to speak clearly and sound natural — especially in **simplified Indonesian** (without prefixes or suffixes).

---

## ❗ Quick Comparison

| English Sentence           | Indonesian (Simplified)      | Why?                        |
|----------------------------|-------------------------------|-----------------------------|
| I **don’t eat** meat.      | Saya **tidak** makan daging.  | “makan” = verb              |
| This is **not** my book.   | Ini **bukan** buku saya.      | “buku” = noun               |
| He is **not** tired.       | Dia **tidak** capek.          | “capek” = adjective         |
| That’s **not** true.       | Itu **bukan** benar.          | “benar” = noun idea         |

---

## 🧠 Rule Summary

### ✅ Use **tidak** for:
- Verbs: makan (eat), tidur (sleep), pergi (go)
- Adjectives: capek (tired), senang (happy)

### ✅ Use **bukan** for:
- Nouns: buku, orang, makanan
- Facts or identity (like “not a doctor”)

---

|-----------------------------|-------------------------------|

## ❌ Examples with **bukan**

| English                     | Indonesian (Simplified)       |
|-----------------------------|-------------------------------|
| That’s not my phone.        | Itu **bukan** HP saya.        |
| I’m not a teacher.          | Saya **bukan** guru.          |
| She’s not your friend.      | Dia **bukan** teman kamu.     |
| This is not food.           | Ini **bukan** makanan.        |

---

## 💬 Everyday Speaking Tip

In casual talk, people say **nggak** or **ga** instead of **tidak**:

- Saya **nggak** tahu. = I don’t know.  
- Dia **ga** suka. = He doesn’t like.

Use them freely when chatting!
