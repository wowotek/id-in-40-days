# Day 1 – Alphabet and Pronunciation
Indonesian uses the Latin alphabet (A–Z), just like English. It has 26 letters, so learning the alphabet is straightforward for English speakers.

Even better: Indonesian is a **phonetic language**. This means words are pronounced exactly the way they are written, every single time. Once you know the sound of a letter, you'll always use that sound. No weird English silent letters or complex rules!

## Special Letter Combinations and Sounds
These letter combinations may look unfamiliar at first, but they’re very common in Indonesian and easy to master.

### ng – nasal "ng" sound
Similar to how 'ng' sound in "si[**ng**]" and "fa[**ng**]" in English

- Examples:
  - ngantuk = sleepy
  - bangun = to wake up
  - manga = manga (Japanese comic)

### ngg – hard "ng + g" sound
Pronounced like "lo[**ng g**]un"

- Examples:
  - nggak = no (informal)
  - bangga = proud
  - mangga = mango

### ny – "nyah" / "nya" sound
Like "nyo" in "canyon" but instead of "nyo" its "nya"

- Examples:
  - nyanyi = to sing
  - banyak = many

### sy – "sh" sound
From Arabic origin words, sounds like "sh" in "shoe"

- Examples:
  - syukur = gratitude
  - syarat = condition

### kh – deep throat "h" sound
A sound made from the back of the throat, like Arabic خ (khā’)

- Examples:
  - khalifah = caliph (Islamic leader)
  - akhir = end

this sound is safe to assume and use the normal "h" sound of english

## Sentence Examples
Remember our one rule: **SVO (Subject – Verb – Object/Rest)**. This is how you will speak Indonesian! Use these sentences for vocalization practice.


### 1. Aku makan nasi.
**I eat rice.**
- **Aku** = I / me
- **makan** = to eat
- **nasi** = rice

### 2. Kamu minum air.
**You drink water.**
- **Kamu** = you
- **minum** = to drink
- **air** = water

### 3. Dia beli buku.
**He/She buys a book.**
- **Dia** = he / she
- **beli** = to buy
- **buku** = book
