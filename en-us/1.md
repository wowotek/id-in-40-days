# Day 1 – Alphabet and Pronunciation
Indonesian uses the Latin alphabet (A–Z), just like English. It has 26 letters, so learning the alphabet is straightforward for English speakers.

Indonesian is a phonetic language, which means that words are pronounced just the way they are written. Each letter usually represents one specific sound, and that sound stays the same no matter where the letter appears in a word. This makes reading and pronunciation infinitely easier than in English!

## Special Letter Combinations and Sounds
These letter combinations may look unfamiliar at first, but they’re very common in Indonesian and easy to master.

### ng – nasal “ng” sound
Similar to how 'ng' sound in “sing” and "finger" in English

- Examples:
  - ngantuk = sleepy
  - bangun = to wake up
  - manga = manga (Japanese comic)

### ngg – hard “ng + g” sound
Pronounced like “finger” or “long gun”

- Examples:
  - nggak = no (informal)
  - bangga = proud
  - mangga = mango

### ny – “nyah” / “nya” sound
Like “canyon” or “onion”

- Examples:
  - nyanyi = to sing
  - banyak = many

### sy – “sh” sound
From Arabic origin words, sounds like “sh” in “shoe”

- Examples:
  - syukur = gratitude
  - syarat = condition

### kh – deep throat “h” sound
A sound made from the back of the throat, like Arabic خ (khā’)

- Examples:
  - khalifah = caliph (Islamic leader)
  - akhir = end

this sound is safe to assume and use the normal "h" sound of english
