# Day 7 – Question Words ❓

To have a conversation, you need to ask questions! Today, you'll learn the core **question words** in Bahasa Indonesia and how to use them.

These are essential tools for navigating daily life — from asking directions to ordering food or learning about someone.

---

## 🧩 Key Question Words

| English       | Indonesian  | Example                           |
|---------------|-------------|-----------------------------------|
| What          | **Apa**     | Apa ini? → What is this?          |
| Who           | **Siapa**   | Siapa dia? → Who is he/she?       |
| Where         | **Di mana** | Kamu tinggal **di mana**? → Where do you live? |
| When          | **Kapan**   | Kamu datang **kapan**? → When will you come? |
| Why           | **Kenapa** / **Mengapa** | Kenapa kamu sedih? → Why are you sad? |
| How           | **Bagaimana** | **Bagaimana** kabarmu? → How are you? |
| How many / much | **Berapa** | **Berapa** harganya? → How much is it? |

---

## 🧠 Structure Tip

In Indonesian, **question words usually go at the end** of the sentence — especially in casual speech.

Compare:

- **Where do you live?**  
  → Kamu tinggal **di mana**?  
- **What is your name?**  
  → Nama kamu **siapa**?

You *can* place the question word first for emphasis or formal use:  
→ **Siapa** nama kamu?

---

## 🗣️ Sample Sentences

| English                        | Indonesian                          |
|--------------------------------|-------------------------------------|
| What is this?                  | Ini **apa**?                        |
| Who is your teacher?           | **Siapa** guru kamu?                |
| Where do you work?             | Kamu kerja **di mana**?            |
| When is your birthday?         | **Kapan** ulang tahun kamu?         |
| Why are you late?              | **Kenapa** kamu telat?              |
| How do you go to school?       | **Bagaimana** kamu ke sekolah?      |
| How much is this?              | Ini **berapa**?                     |

---

## 📣 Common Alternatives

- **Kenapa** and **mengapa** both mean *why*.  
  - “Kenapa” is more casual.  
  - “Mengapa” is formal/literary.
  
- You might hear **dimana** as one word, but the correct form is **di mana** (preposition + question word).
