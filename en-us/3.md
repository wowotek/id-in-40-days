# Day 3 – Personal Pronouns (I, You, We…)

Understanding **personal pronouns** is essential to forming basic sentences. Indonesian pronouns are **straightforward**, **gender-neutral**, and **do not change with grammar tense** — which makes them super easy to learn.

---

## 👤 Singular Pronouns

| English     | Indonesian        | Notes                                                  |
|-------------|-------------------|--------------------------------------------------------|
| I / Me      | **Saya** / **Aku** | "Saya" is formal / polite. "Aku" is casual / close.    |
| You         | **Kamu** / **Anda** | "Kamu" is casual. "Anda" is formal / polite.           |
| He / She    | **Dia**            | No gender distinction.                                 |
| It          | *(not used directly)* | Indonesian rarely uses "it". Use **itu** = *that*.     |

---

## 👥 Plural Pronouns

| English        | Indonesian | Notes                                                    |
|----------------|------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| We (inclusive) | **Kita**   | Includes the person you're talking to.                  |
| We (exclusive) | **Kami**   | Excludes the person you're talking to.                  |
| You (plural)   | **Kalian** | Informal plural “you all”                               |
| They           | **Mereka** | For any group of people                                 |

> 🧠 There are no separate masculine/feminine forms. “Dia” works for both he/she.

---

## 🗣️ Sample Sentences

- **Saya suka kopi.** → I like coffee.  
- **Dia pintar.** → He/She is smart.  
- **Kami pergi ke pasar.** → We (not you) are going to the market.  
- **Kita belajar bersama.** → We (including you) are studying together.  
- **Kalian lucu sekali.** → You all are very funny.  
- **Mereka tinggal di Jakarta.** → They live in Jakarta.

---

## 🔄 Quick Comparison: Saya vs Aku

| Feature        | Saya              | Aku                  |
|----------------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Formality      | Polite / neutral  | Casual / emotional   |
| Usage context  | Business, writing, strangers | Friends, family, partners |
| Safe for...    | Any situation     | Close relationships  |

---

## 👀 Cultural Note

- “**Anda**” is formal, respectful, and distant — used in customer service, advertising, or official speech.  
- In everyday conversation, most people use **"kamu"**, or replace pronouns with names/titles like **mas**, **mbak**, or just the person's name — especially in Javanese-speaking regions.
